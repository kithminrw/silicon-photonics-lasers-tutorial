Four things to try

  1. Unlock the phases. Press random: the spectrum does not change at all, but the 0.63 ps pulses dissolve into noise, and the peak falls from 74 times the mean power to only a few times (press new random phases to see it vary). A spectrum analyser cannot tell the two lasers apart.
  2. Widen the bandwidth. Take it from 4 to 8 nm: the pulses shorten from 0.63 to 0.32 ps and the time–bandwidth product stays at 0.44, the limit for a Gaussian pulse.
  3. Lengthen the cavity. Double it to 4.28 mm: the pulses come at 10 GHz instead of 20, twice as many lines fit in the same 4 nm, and the pulse keeps its width, so its peak doubles to 149 times the mean.
  4. Add chirp. At 0.5 ps² the pulse spreads to 2.3 ps, with a time–bandwidth product of 1.6. Real semiconductor mode-locked lasers emit chirped pulses; a dispersive element outside the laser can compress them again.
