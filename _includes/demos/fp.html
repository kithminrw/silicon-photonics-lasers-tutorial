Three things to try

  1. Raise the mirrors. On Bragg mirrors, take both reflectances from 0.30 to 0.95: the finesse climbs from 2.2 to 53 and the resonance narrows to 32 pm. The 2 dB/cm of loss, harmless at 0.69, now costs a quarter of the peak (76 %), because the light makes many more passes before it leaves.
  2. Unbalance them. Set the loss to 0 and R₂ to 0.60 with R₁ at 0.95: the peak transmission falls to 33 % and the reflection no longer vanishes on resonance. Lower R₁ until R₁ = R₂ and the peak returns to 100 %, at any reflectance. Now raise the loss to 20 dB/cm: the reflection vanishes only when R₁ = R₂e−2αL, and even then part of the light is absorbed.
  3. Pump the laser chip. On cleaved InP, drag the loss below zero into gain. At 110 dB/cm of gain the peaks rise to 90 times the input and narrow to 34 pm; near 124 dB/cm (28.5 cm⁻¹) the round trip gives ρ = 1 and the chip lases: the threshold of Chapter 24.
