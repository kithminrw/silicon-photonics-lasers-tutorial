Four things to try

  1. Design off centre. Move the design coupling to 14.5 %, as Chapter 14's Lorentzian estimate suggested: every die fails the crosstalk limit, and so do all eight corners. The yield curve shows the window of designs that work.
  2. Watch the corners turn pessimistic. Back at 11.15 %, raise the independent gap error to 3 nm: four of the eight corners fail, yet with 10 000 dies about 99.7 % pass the Monte Carlo. The corners stack all three errors at three sigma at once, which almost never happens.
  3. Count the error bar. Still with the 3 nm gap error, set 100 dies: the estimate is uncertain by about a per cent and cannot tell 99 % from 100 %. With 10 000 dies it is good to about 0.06 %. Press new dies at 100 to watch the estimate jump.
  4. Find the tolerant direction. Set the thickness and gap errors to 0 and raise the width bias to 10 nm: the spread hardly grows. Wider waveguides couple less, a narrower gap couples more, and in this design the two nearly cancel (Section 29.4).
