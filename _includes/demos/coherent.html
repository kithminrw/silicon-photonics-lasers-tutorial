Three things to try

  1. Drive harder. On 16QAM, raise the swing from 0.5 to 1.0 Vπ. The sine of the MZM squeezes the outer levels towards the inner ones, so the grid is no longer even, and the modulation loss falls from 5.5 to 2.0 dB, but the outer-to-inner field ratio drops from 2.7 to 2.0 instead of 3. Somewhere between lies the best trade.
  2. Find the noise floor. Lower the SNR until bit errors appear: QPSK survives to about 10 dB, 16QAM needs about 17 dB for the same error rate. Two more bits per symbol cost about 7 dB.
  3. Spoil the 90°. Set an I–Q phase error of 10° and then a phase noise of 5° rms at 20 dB SNR. 16QAM's outer corners smear first, because a rotation moves them furthest; QPSK hardly notices. Coherent DSP measures and removes both.
