Three things to try

  1. Reproduce Worked Example 19.2. Switch to one arm: Δn is 1.51 × 10⁻⁵ per volt and VπL 5.12 V·cm. Push–pull halves it to 2.56 V·cm, so the 10 mm device needs 2.56 V for a π shift. The field solve's overlap at this 5 µm gap, 0.52, is close to the 0.5 the example assumes.
  2. Close the gap to 2.5 µm. At Γ = 0.5, VπL halves to 1.28 V·cm, better than silicon's 1.37. The solve's overlap rises to 0.58; set Γ to it and VπL is 1.10 V·cm. But the electrode corners now sit where the mode still has 0.27 % of its peak intensity, and the metal there absorbs.
  3. Switch to BTO on Si. At the same gap and Γ = 0.5, VπL drops to 0.21 V·cm, and the loss jumps to a typical 3 dB/cm, so αVπL is 0.64 dB·V. Only part of the mode is in the BTO: at Γ = 0.2 VπL is 0.53 V·cm, still under the 1 V·cm of Table 19.2.
