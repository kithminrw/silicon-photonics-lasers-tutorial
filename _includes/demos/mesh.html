Three things to try

  1. Grow the mesh. Set the phase error to 0.1 rad, switch from 4 × 4 to 8 × 8 and press new chip a few times. The light now crosses twice as many MZIs: averaged over many chips the fidelity falls from about 97 % to 93 %, and the worst entry's error more than doubles, from about 5 to 11 points of power. One chip can be luckier than the average: the spread between chips is as large as the trend.
  2. Add splitter errors. Set the phase error to 0 and the splitter error to 5 %: each coupler splits 45:55 or so instead of 50:50, and the MZIs can no longer reach full bar or full cross. Press new target a few times: the fidelity depends on the target, because matrices that need full switching suffer most. On average it is about 99.3 % for a 4 × 4 mesh and 97 % for an 8 × 8.
  3. Make another chip. Press new chip: the same σ gives a different set of errors, and a different fidelity. Large meshes are calibrated one by one after fabrication, or set up by self-configuring algorithms that correct for their own errors.
