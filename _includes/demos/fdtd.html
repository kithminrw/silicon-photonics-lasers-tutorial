Three things to try

  1. Break the Courant limit. Set S to 0.71, just above 1/√2 = 0.707: the pill turns red and within about 300 steps a checkerboard, the finest pattern the grid can hold, grows out of round-off in the oxide and swamps the picture. The energy plot shows it growing ×1.42 per step (×1.44 on an unbounded grid). At 0.70 the run stays stable however long it goes.
  2. Turn off the PML. The grid now ends in a metal wall: the wave comes back from the far end, the energy keeps climbing and the readings never settle. Turn it back on and vary its thickness: the guided wave’s far-end reflection is −49 dB with 4 cells, −74 dB with 6 and −90 dB with the default 12.
  3. Coarsen the grid. In the straight guide, go from N = 12 to 6: neff rises from 2.5115 to 2.5844, 3.7 % above the exact 2.492, so the wavelength in the guide shrinks from 617 to 600 nm (622 nm exact). In the bend at R = 1 µm the transmission moves from 88.4 % to 90.2 % (MEEP: 88.3 %). A ring resonance moves further: a pulse run puts it at 1585.8 nm with N = 12 and 1596.8 nm with N = 8.
