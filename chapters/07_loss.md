# <span class="chapnum">Chapter 7</span>Loss, Dispersion and Nonlinearity

A waveguide's job is to get light from A to B unchanged; this chapter is about everything that gets in the way.
{: .chapterlead }

<p><span class="status status-outline">outline</span> This chapter is an outline: the sections, demos and reading are planned, and the text is still to be written.</p>

<div class="tbox tbox-objectives" markdown="1">
<div class="tbox-title">In this chapter you will learn to</div>

- classify loss mechanisms: sidewall scattering, absorption, substrate leakage and radiation;
- estimate scattering loss with the Payne–Lacey model;
- include free-carrier and two-photon absorption;
- describe material and waveguide dispersion;
- choose a loss-measurement method: cutback, spirals, rings or reflectometry.

</div>

<div class="tbox tbox-insight" markdown="1">
<div class="tbox-title">Design question</div>

What limits a waveguide's propagation loss, and how do you measure it?

</div>

## <span class="secnum">7.1</span>Loss mechanisms and units

dB/cm, nepers and the loss budget.

## <span class="secnum">7.2</span>Sidewall-roughness scattering

The Payne–Lacey model; why wider waveguides lose less; TE against TM.

## <span class="secnum">7.3</span>Absorption

Material absorption, free carriers from doping, and metals near the mode.

## <span class="secnum">7.4</span>Substrate leakage

BOX thickness, O band against C band, SiN.

## <span class="secnum">7.5</span>Dispersion

Material and waveguide dispersion, group-velocity dispersion and dispersion engineering.

## <span class="secnum">7.6</span>Nonlinear effects

Kerr effect, two-photon and free-carrier absorption in silicon, power limits; SiN for combs.

## <span class="secnum">7.7</span>Measuring loss

Cutback, spirals, ring-based extraction and reflectometry, with their uncertainties.
