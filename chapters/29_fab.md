# <span class="chapnum">Chapter 29</span>Fabrication

A photonic chip is made with the same tools as a microprocessor, but its devices care about nanometres in different ways.
{: .chapterlead }

<p><span class="status status-outline">outline</span> This chapter is an outline: the sections, demos and reading are planned, and the text is still to be written.</p>

<div class="tbox tbox-objectives" markdown="1">
<div class="tbox-title">In this chapter you will learn to</div>

- describe SOI wafer manufacture and the main process steps: lithography, etch, cladding, doping, metals, germanium;
- compare DUV lithography with electron-beam lithography;
- describe SiN deposition, TFLN etching and III–V bonding;
- turn width and thickness variations into wavelength shifts;
- apply design-for-manufacturing practices and choose an MPW route.

</div>

<div class="tbox tbox-insight" markdown="1">
<div class="tbox-title">Design question</div>

How do nanometre variations turn into nanometre wavelength shifts?

</div>

Smart Cut and film-thickness uniformity.

Full and partial etch, sidewall angle, oxide cladding.

Implants, contacts, heaters and Ge epitaxy.

SiN deposition, TFLN etching, III–V bonding.

## <span class="secnum">29.6</span>Process variation

Wafer maps, corners and their spectral consequences.

Robust designs, fill, and tolerant devices.
